# Ten lateral paths to the north star, prioritized (November 1, 2026)

Operator request, November 1: research online, add ten lateral paths to the
north star, prioritize all of them, and take concrete steps. This page records
the result. It follows the [learning procedure](../world-model/LEARNING.md):
each path borrows a mechanism from another context, names what transfers and
what does not, and states the cheapest discriminating test. An analogy here
generates a hypothesis. It never establishes demand, effect or authority.

The north star is unchanged: measurable, positive, spendable human earnings and
broader access to useful economic opportunity. Credit is provisional. The
lending qualification found NONE QUALIFIES and the agent-loan attempt is
stopped. No path below asks for money, contact or deployment authority. Every
path is compared against the revolving first-job payment guarantee in
[NORTH_STAR.md](NORTH_STAR.md), its two scorecards (reserve survival `E[Δ reserve]`
and sponsor multiplier `M = B / sponsor money consumed`), and its exposure rules.

Model state read: main `fc0d8cb`, model `0.1.197`. Lessons recalled before
writing: `action:qualify-revolving-guarantee-channel-20261011` (no channel
qualified; escrow was the simpler control; the documented first-job barrier on
protected platforms is the hiring decision, not nonpayment) and
`action:agent-loan-borrower-qualification-20261029` (distribution failure, no
qualified borrower; prepayment and capacity alternatives remain open).

## Ranking criteria

Each path is scored 0 to 3 on the five criteria in LEARNING.md step 5. Ties are
broken by credible human benefit, then by ability to execute now within existing
authority. Scores are judgments made on November 1 from the sources listed below.
They are not probabilities and must be revised when a cheapest test returns.

| Criterion | 0 | 3 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Credible human benefit | No causal evidence of earnings or access effect | Randomized evidence of earnings or employment effect in a comparable barrier |
| Ability to execute now | Needs money, contact or an account we do not have | Zero spend, zero contact, capability already demonstrated by this team |
| Useful evidence | The cheapest test teaches little | The cheapest test resolves a named parameter or kills the path |
| Full costs and downside | Spend, legal exposure or harm risk | Near-zero cost, reversible, no participant risk |
| Reusable value | Nothing carries to other paths | Reuses the contract, review capability or evidence base |

## The ten paths

Each entry states: mechanism and where it worked; the measurable human outcome
or decision; the evidence sought; the competing simpler route; the relation to
the guarantee; the transfer limit; the cheapest test within existing authority;
and the stop or change condition.

### L1. Verifiable skill signals and public work evaluations

**Mechanism and source context.** In Johannesburg, randomly adding a public,
verifiable skill-assessment certificate to real job applications raised
employment from 31 to 36 percent and earnings by 34 percent; the three-month
earnings gain was 2.3 times the variable cost of assessing and certifying, and a
private, unverifiable certificate had no employment effect
(Carranza, Garlick, Orkin and Rankin, AER 2022, as summarized by J-PAL). On
oDesk, hiring 952 inexperienced workers for one ten-hour job and publishing a
detailed evaluation substantially raised later employment; a summary reports
that earnings roughly tripled over two months (Pallais, AER 2014). In South
Africa, a standardized reference letter raised employer callbacks from 4.2 to
6.7 percent and employment for women by 13 percentage points; information about
the letter's value worked and money did not (Abel, Burger and Piraino, AEJ
Applied 2020). On an online freelancing platform, voluntary skill certificates
raised earnings mainly through higher-value projects, with selection caveats
(Kässi and Lehdonvirta, CESifo 2019).

**Human outcome or decision.** A newcomer's first paid job and earnings in the
following months, caused by a credible signal rather than by credit.

**Evidence sought.** Whether any channel reaching newcomers will display a
third-party verifiable signal, and what a first-job completion record would
need to contain to be that signal.

**Competing simpler route.** The platform's own tests and badges. If a channel
already certifies newcomers verifiably, we add nothing.

**Relation to the guarantee.** Same population, different barrier. The
guarantee's completed-and-paid job record in GuaranteeTrust is itself a public
evaluation. A signal needs no reserve, so `E[Δ reserve]` and ruin do not apply;
`M` is benefit divided by assessment cost, which the Johannesburg study bounds
at 2.3 for three months in that context.

**Transfer limit.** The RCTs ran with large placement programmes and a public
employment service. Effects depend on employer trust in the issuer; our issuer
has no reputation. Displacement of other newcomers is possible and the oDesk
study could not rule it out.

**Cheapest test.** Zero contact: confirm from public documentation that at
least one newcomer channel displays partner-issued credentials, and record its
partner terms. Then write a one-page specification of a verifiable completed-work
attestation derived from existing contract events, with the issuer-trust problem
stated. **Receipt, November 1:** Upwork's help center describes a Partner
Certified Talent programme in which partner-issued, partner-validated
certifications appear on freelancer profiles and proposals. Partner eligibility
terms were not retrieved. This is channel existence, not acceptance of us.

**Stop or change.** Stop if no channel displays external credentials to the
hiring party, or if the only channels that do already certify newcomers at no
cost. Change to L3 if a placement programme would use the signal only with its
own clients.

### L2. Rules-as-code maintenance for benefits calculators

**Mechanism and source context.** Information plus application assistance
raised SNAP enrollment among likely-eligible older adults to about 18 percent,
against about 11 percent with information alone and 6 percent in control
(Finkelstein and Notowidigdo, QJE 2019, as summarized; verify against the
published tables). Benefits navigators depend on calculators whose rules are
code. This team has already corrected PolicyEngine US rules on maintainer
request; one correction was merged upstream, though incorporation of our cases
into any household's result was not shown.

**Human outcome or decision.** Fewer wrong eligibility or amount results shown
to households and advisers who already use the calculator.

**Evidence sought.** A current maintainer-filed defect, an accepted correction,
and any downstream evidence that the corrected rule reaches a navigator or
household.

**Competing simpler route.** Maintainers fix their own backlog. Our
contribution is additive only where the backlog is unassigned and the fix is
accepted.

**Relation to the guarantee.** None mechanically. It multiplies an existing
public tool instead of creating a pool. `M` is undefined because no sponsor
money is consumed; the cost is reviewer time.

**Transfer limit.** The SNAP study measured assistance by a trusted nonprofit,
not calculator accuracy. Accuracy gains reach households only through a
navigator's use, which we cannot observe without a partner.

**Cheapest test.** Zero contact: scan the upstream tracker for current,
unassigned, maintainer-filed defects. **Receipt, November 1 (19:36 to 19:40
UTC, GitHub search API):** PolicyEngine US has 0 open issues labeled help
wanted and 8 labeled good first issue, all last updated in 2023 or 2024, so
label-based discovery yields nothing current. The same tracker has 156 open,
unassigned issues updated since October 1, 2026, filed by maintainer accounts,
most with zero comments; one example is issue 10552, labeled bug, filed
October 30 (Schedule R initial amount for separate filers). A secondary rate
limit ended the scan. This is a lead inventory, not an assignment. Any
submission follows the existing gates: Codex selection, non-author review,
and the October 18 stop on further territory-reimbursement submissions until a
maintainer responds.

**Stop or change.** Stop if two reviewed corrections in a row receive no
maintainer response within their observation windows, or if no route to a
navigator's actual use can be named by the next checkpoint.

### L3. Cooperative or placement programme channel with its own clients

**Mechanism and source context.** Up & Go, a New York cleaning platform owned
by worker cooperatives, reports that workers keep 95 percent of each booking
and earn about 22 dollars an hour against about 10 dollars before joining;
annual platform profit was about 5,000 dollars (self-reported and advocacy
coverage). A programme that places newcomers with its own clients is the one
channel type in NORTH_STAR.md step 0 that was not disqualified by escrow.

**Human outcome or decision.** Whether a real channel has uncovered client
nonpayment on first jobs, which decides whether the guarantee has any
qualifying channel at all.

**Evidence sought.** Public dispute, cancellation or nonpayment data from
cooperative or placement platforms; their onboarding bottleneck for newcomers.

**Competing simpler route.** The cooperative's own client prepayment or
booking deposit. If clients prepay, `p` is near zero and the guarantee is
unnecessary.

**Relation to the guarantee.** Direct: this is the channel qualification step
with a specific channel type. If `p` is measurable here, the exposure rules
(`G` at most a fifth of the reserve, one open guarantee per client) apply.

**Transfer limit.** Cooperatives are member-owned; a guarantor outside the
cooperative may be unwanted or legally awkward. The earnings figures are
self-reported.

**Cheapest test.** Zero contact: read public terms and reports of three
platform cooperatives or placement programmes for payment timing, deposits and
dispute handling; record whether any publishes a nonpayment rate. Check the
October 11 screen's coverage first so the test is not a repeat.

**Stop or change.** Stop if all three prepay or hold deposits. Change to L1 if
their documented bottleneck is client trust in a newcomer rather than payment.

### L4. Open-source tax-filing continuation for unclaimed EITC

**Mechanism and source context.** IRS estimates 8.2 billion dollars of Earned
Income Tax Credit went unclaimed for tax year 2021, 5.6 billion of it by about
4.6 million eligible non-filers. The IRS published most of Direct File's code
in 2025 under the SHARE IT Act, told states the service will not be available
in 2026, and the code depends on internal IRS systems; a Code for America fork
and a community self-hosted continuation are reported.

**Human outcome or decision.** Spendable refunds reaching eligible non-filers
through a free filing route that a state or nonprofit operates.

**Evidence sought.** An active fork with maintainer-filed issues and a named
operator planning to serve filers, so that reviewed code work could reach a
household.

**Competing simpler route.** Existing free-file alliance software and VITA
sites. Program-level VITA data show gains but no controlled evidence.

**Relation to the guarantee.** None. Same review capability as L2.

**Transfer limit.** A refund is spendable income but not earned income from
work. Non-filer outreach has weak evidence; broad campaigns did little. Code
that cannot run outside IRS systems may never serve a filer.

**Cheapest test.** Zero contact: identify the active fork, its last commit date
and its open maintainer-filed issues. **Receipt, November 1:** status of the
upstream release recorded from public reporting; fork activity not yet checked
because the GitHub search budget was spent on L2.

**Stop or change.** Stop if no fork has a named operator for the 2026 filing
season.

### L5. Nonpayment recovery through existing law

**Mechanism and source context.** New York City's Freelance Isn't Free Act
produced over 3.5 million dollars recovered across more than 4,300 closed cases
since 2017, roughly 800 dollars per closed case (city remarks, May 2026).
Invoice data from more than 100,000 freelancers show 29 percent of invoices paid
at least a day late (Bonsai); 56 percent of US small businesses report unpaid
invoices outstanding (Intuit QuickBooks 2025 survey).

**Human outcome or decision.** Owed wages actually recovered by a worker who
would otherwise have written them off.

**Evidence sought.** Jurisdictions with a free recovery route, their recovery
per complaint and time to resolution, and whether workers fail to use them for
lack of information.

**Competing simpler route.** The agency's own outreach and the freelancers'
union guides. If those already reach the workers, an information artifact adds
nothing. The October 28 recovery proposal's limits apply: information-only
deliverables, no case files, no filing on anyone's behalf.

**Relation to the guarantee.** Substitute. Recovery after the fact competes
with insurance before the fact; where a free route recovers most owed wages,
the guarantee's benefit shrinks. The late-payment rates are not `p`, which is
final nonpayment.

**Transfer limit.** One city's law; recovery figures are cumulative and
include large settlements. Legal and personal-data work is not authorized.

**Cheapest test.** Zero contact: a source-linked map of freelancer payment laws
and published recovery statistics, with the explicit gap between late payment
and final nonpayment.

**Stop or change.** Stop if published recovery rates are high and awareness is
not the documented gap.

### L6. Member-funded mutual pool with no sponsor capital

**Mechanism and source context.** Village savings and loan associations pool
members' own savings and lend without outside capital. Randomized trials in
Ghana, Malawi and Uganda found more savings, more loans, more profitable
businesses and greater women's empowerment, but no effect on household income
or food security (Karlan and colleagues, PNAS 2017).

**Human outcome or decision.** Whether a member-owned pool can give newcomers
the guarantee's protection without a sponsor whose loss must be multiplied.

**Evidence sought.** Simulated survival of a member-funded pool under the
existing `ruin.py` assumptions, and whether any newcomer group would fund it.

**Competing simpler route.** Members simply saving individually. The RCTs
suggest the pool's value is access and discipline, not income.

**Relation to the guarantee.** Changes the payer. With zero sponsor capital `M`
is undefined and the only scorecard is reserve survival. The GuaranteeTrust
contract's `totalDues` already separates member contributions.

**Transfer limit.** Village groups rely on face-to-face trust and weekly
meetings; the RCTs show no income effect; recruiting members is contact we do
not have.

**Cheapest test.** Zero spend: one simulation run with sponsor capital set to
zero and contributions as the only inflow, reported beside the existing 39 to
51 percent and 0.1 to 3.6 percent ruin results.

**Stop or change.** Stop if survival requires contribution rates that erode
worker benefit, as the existing stop condition already states.

### L7. First-wage subsidy voucher as the direct-sponsorship comparator

**Mechanism and source context.** A randomized wage-subsidy voucher in South
Africa raised wage employment by about 7 percentage points after one year, with
the gain persisting at two years, even though few firms claimed the subsidy
(Levinsohn, Rankin, Roberts and Schöer). In the United States, Grameen
America's randomized evaluation found business earnings up 127 dollars a month
with a modest, less definitive net-income effect (MDRC 2022).

**Human outcome or decision.** Whether a sponsor dollar spent directly on a
first wage beats a sponsor dollar held in a guarantee reserve.

**Evidence sought.** A computed `M` for a voucher under the two-scorecard
definitions, using the RCT effect size and a stated displacement assumption,
beside the guarantee's `M` at the same `p` and `c`.

**Competing simpler route.** Direct cash, with about 80 to 89 percent of funds
delivered to recipients in the best-known programme (self-reported; GiveWell's
estimate is about 83 percent).

**Relation to the guarantee.** This is the direct-sponsorship comparison that
NORTH_STAR.md requires every cycle. A voucher consumes sponsor money by design;
the guarantee hopes not to.

**Transfer limit.** Youth labor market in one country; voucher take-up by firms
was thin; a US employer channel and sponsor money do not exist here.

**Cheapest test.** Zero spend: the `M` comparison as a reproducible note in the
theory repository, with every assumption named.

**Stop or change.** If the voucher's `M` exceeds the guarantee's across the
plausible range, the guarantee is no longer the candidate to pilot.

### L8. Payment timing instead of nonpayment insurance

**Mechanism and source context.** Earned wage access advances wages already
earned. The CFPB's December 2025 advisory opinion treats certain products as
not credit; a University of Oregon study reports reduced reliance on overdrafts
and payday loans with about one insufficient-funds fee per 15 uses; a South
Carolina regulator found annual user costs from 0.68 to 218.83 dollars across
13 providers in 2024.

**Human outcome or decision.** Whether newcomers' binding problem is when they
are paid rather than whether they are paid.

**Evidence sought.** Payment-timing terms on newcomer channels and the cost of
existing timing products, compared with the team's resource-first cash-timing
worksheet.

**Competing simpler route.** Faster platform payout or client milestone
payments at no cost to the worker.

**Relation to the guarantee.** A guarantee that pays the worker at the due
date and collects from the client later is a timing product; it needs the same
reserve and inherits credit-adjacent regulation.

**Transfer limit.** Evidence is employer-integrated payroll in the United
States, much of it industry-funded; harms and fees are contested.

**Cheapest test.** Zero contact: a public-terms comparison of payout timing on
three newcomer channels with the regulator's cost range.

**Stop or change.** Stop if channels already pay within days at no fee.

### L9. Positive payment-history reporting for credit building

**Mechanism and source context.** Rent reporting to credit bureaus is reported
to raise scores by 46 points on average and to have created 27,393 new scores
for previously credit-invisible renters (company-reported, no control group).

**Human outcome or decision.** Whether a newcomer's completed-and-paid work
history could become a bureau-reportable tradeline that widens access to
ordinary credit.

**Evidence sought.** Furnisher requirements for small data furnishers and any
independent evaluation of positive-only reporting.

**Competing simpler route.** Existing rent and utility reporting services.

**Relation to the guarantee.** Reuses the contract's payment record as a
reputation asset instead of a loss-absorbing pool.

**Transfer limit.** Bureau agreements, dispute handling and consumer-reporting
law are far from this team's authority; the evidence is self-reported.

**Cheapest test.** Zero contact: a public-source note on furnisher eligibility
and independent evidence, to decide whether the path is even reachable.

**Stop or change.** Stop if furnishing requires a regulated entity we cannot
be.

### L10. Job-search cost subsidy

**Mechanism and source context.** In Addis Ababa, a transport subsidy raised
formal employment in the short run; later follow-up found the effect dissipated
while the skill-certification workshop's effects persisted (Abebe and
colleagues; already recorded as roadmap source S7).

**Human outcome or decision.** Whether removing a small, specific search cost
produces a first job.

**Evidence sought.** A channel where a named search cost blocks an otherwise
likely hire.

**Competing simpler route.** Employer-paid travel or remote work.

**Relation to the guarantee.** A direct sponsor transfer with no reserve.

**Transfer limit.** Different labor market; effects fade; requires spend and a
consenting worker.

**Cheapest test.** None within current authority beyond what the roadmap
already records; this path waits for a channel from L1 or L3.

**Stop or change.** Remains parked unless a channel names a search cost.

## Considered and set aside

- **Lend through an existing zero-interest platform.** Kiva US reports high
  repayment but no independent additionality evaluation was found. It is
  credit again, which the lending qualification already answered.
- **Retroactive public-goods funding as capital formation.** Programme terms
  for 2026 were not confirmed, and raising sponsor capital before a route
  clears `M > 1` inverts the north star's order.
- **Direct cash.** Remains the benchmark, not a path; its delivery efficiency
  is recorded under L7.

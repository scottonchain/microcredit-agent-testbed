## Measurable outcome, evidence, simpler route, stop condition

- **Human outcome or decision:** a person in poverty can take, use and repay a small loan in her own currency, through a conversation in her own language on a phone she already uses, with the pool's USDC never visible to her; the decision this lane serves is the operator's choice of country and partner type for the first human pilot, and D16's packet. Scorecard unchanged: `M = B / sponsor money consumed`, `B` measured on earnings.
- **Evidence sought:** (a) a priced round trip of 25 USDC into one local currency and back through a licensed provider, every fee and delay recorded; (b) a Spanish-language, voice-capable servicing agent wired to the test app's ledger, tried by named testers through one simulated cycle, with completion, cost per conversation and number-error rate measured; (c) a written reading of Meta's WhatsApp rules against the design, with a demonstrated non-Meta fallback; (d) a partner packet a Friendship Bridge-type organisation could evaluate, held unsent until the operator's word.
- **Competing simpler routes:** the partner's existing process with a plain SMS reminder carrying the facilitator's name (the cheapest proven thing); a licensed partner lending from its own balance sheet with the pool as ledger only (D8); direct cash; NO_LOAN. Each is compared in the packet on the same cases.
- **Stop or change condition:** Meta declines or blocks the account and the fallback cannot carry a pilot; a partner declines twice; the measured round-trip cost exceeds 10 percent of a 10 dollar loan (D16's bound); testers complete fewer than half of a simulated cycle; the agent misstates a number in sampled conversations more than once in a hundred; counsel finds that servicing itself is unlicensed activity in the named country; or any D16 or D7 stop.

## Three findings that shape the design (from the packet)

1. **The borrower never sees a dollar, a token or the word crypto.** Loans are priced, disbursed and repaid in local currency on the rail that currency already uses; conversion to and from USDC happens once, in bulk, on the platform's or a licensed partner's side; the pool, never the borrower, bears exchange-rate risk, and open exposure in any one currency is capped at a fifth of the reserve until hedged. The Zimbabwe precedent (dollar-indexed microloans after a currency collapse) is the harm this rule prevents.
2. **Guatemala first for the partner route, Kenya first for the rails measurement.** The geography screen (need, reach, local rail, lawful stablecoin leg, currency stability, lending rules, harm climate) puts Guatemala at the top because its borrower side can be built without any crypto rule (bank deposit by the Trust Bank, quetzal inside about 1 percent, a model partner type, a WhatsApp-literate client base), and Kenya at the top for measuring a round trip because its off-ramp APIs and VASP regime exist today. Ethiopia and Bangladesh are closed for the stablecoin leg; Nigeria and Ghana wait on currency.
3. **The agent is the licensed lender's servicing assistant, on that lender's account, or it is nothing.** Meta's terms (September 23, 2026) bar AI as the primary function of a business account, and its messaging policy bars "peer-to-peer lending", "payday loans", "debt collection" and "real, virtual, or fake currency" as subjects, in Meta's sole discretion. A bank in Peru disburses loans through WhatsApp today; an unlicensed platform talking about staked USDC could not. So the design is a between-meeting assistant for a lender of record, speaking Spanish by voice, confirming terms, dates and receipts, never messaging anyone but the borrower, with SMS, a phone call or a web link as the fallback from day one.

## How this sits with D16's legal analysis of the same day

D16's [legal analysis](../../evidence/human-first-mainnet-20261103/legal-analysis.md) (merged November 3, PR 418) chose a named lender of record, Colorado first, with dollars disbursed and collected by the lender from its own accounts and converted only for its own treasury, never for a borrower. That is the same rule as D17's, applied to dollars: the borrower's money and the pool's USDC meet only on the lender's side. The sequence is therefore: the first mainnet pilot is D16's (a US lender, dollars, the servicing agent on that lender's own account); the partner route of D17 (Guatemala first, quetzales, a Friendship Bridge-type partner as lender of record) is the second stage, and its rails and channel work run now so that the second stage is not a cold start. Nothing in D17 reopens D16's jurisdiction choice.

## Questions of our own, answered in the packet

The packet's section F answers fifteen questions the operator did not ask, among them: whether the agent is a channel or a mechanism (a channel; it changes cost and reach, not `p` or `q`); who bears exchange-rate risk and what it costs by country; what the smallest product is that never says crypto; whether servicing on WhatsApp can avoid Meta's "debt collection" and "peer-to-peer lending" categories; whether the agent replaces the Trust Bank meeting (no); what a client who does not read Spanish needs (voice, a facilitator who speaks K'iche' or Kaqchikel, a path with no smartphone); how consent is recorded on a shared phone; where the data goes and who can read it (Meta can read Cloud API messages, so the chat carries the minimum); the cheapest test with no partner and no money; what harm the agent adds that the loan does not; what would make a partner say yes or no; whether the first user could be the relative abroad; the comparator; the kill conditions; and what scale would look like.

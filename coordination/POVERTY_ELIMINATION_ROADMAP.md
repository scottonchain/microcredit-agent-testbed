## Define the outcome before interpreting a payment

- **Global extreme poverty:** less than $3.00 per person per day in **2021 purchasing-power-parity international dollars**. World Bank's September 22, 2026 update estimates 824.3 million people, 10.1%, in 2024; its 2026 estimate is a nowcast, not a complete observed household census. Never compare a market-dollar wage directly with the PPP line. Use local welfare, household composition and the country's valid price conversion. For local policy, use the national poverty line. Higher international benchmarks are $4.20 and $8.30, also in 2021 PPP. [S1–S2]
- **Broader deprivation:** national/local income adequacy plus food security, housing, health, education, utilities and resilience. UNDP's 2026 MPI covers overlapping health, education and living-standard deprivations across 108 countries; its 1.2 billion estimate is not interchangeable with monetary poverty or a same-year world census. [S3]
- **United States:** report the official measure and, where appropriate, the Supplemental Poverty Measure, which incorporates taxes, noncash benefits, work/medical costs and housing geography. Benefits and tax changes can alter household welfare even when gross wages rise. [S4]
- **Project outcomes:** separate (a) independent accepted and settled paid work; (b) spendable worker earnings net of actual cash costs; (c) incremental net benefit versus a credible counterfactual, including foregone earnings, displacement and harms; (d) household poverty gap/deprivation change; and (e) persistence and prevention of re-entry. One job can establish (a) without establishing (c), (d) or (e). Record unknowns as unknown; consent is required before household measurement.

## Research that changes the route comparison

1. **Credit is conditional, not the default bridge.** The original six randomized microcredit studies do not establish transformative average gains; modest take-up and imprecise effects also caution against claiming universal uselessness. Updated evidence finds stronger effects in some experienced entrepreneurs. Finance only after real demand, net return and a binding capital gap are demonstrated. [S5–S6]
2. **Access and credible skill signals can outperform finance.** The Addis Ababa randomized study compares job-application/skill-certification workshops with transport support. Workshops improved long-run labor outcomes; transport's initial effects dissipated. This supports testing the actual barrier and sustained results, not assuming all job-search assistance works. The population/context differs from current nLine applicants; transferability is unproved. [S7]
3. **Cash is a serious benchmark, even under the multiplier requirement.** Egger et al.'s Kenya experiment reports a local transfer multiplier around 2.5 with positive spillovers. It is a context-specific local economic multiplier, **not** this project's M and not evidence that every cash program exceeds this project's sponsor-benefit threshold. It nevertheless falsifies the blanket premise that a transfer can never multiply economic activity. Compare like outcomes, horizons and costs rather than dismissing cash by label. [S8]
4. **People with multiple constraints may need a bundle.** An Indian Graduation randomized trial shows durable welfare gains over ten years from assets plus complementary supports; its historical PPP outcome values cannot be compared directly with today's $3 line. This is evidence for considering effective livelihoods delivery, not proof the project can reproduce the result or that every household leaves broader poverty. In Honduras, widespread chicken illness destroyed productive assets and the evaluated package did not clear its cost-benefit test, illustrating correlated livelihood loss. [S9–S10]
5. **Elimination requires protection as well as earnings.** ILO's 2024–26 report estimates only 52.4% covered by at least one social-protection benefit, using primarily 2023 coverage data/modeling. Coverage is not adequacy; the report highlights major financing gaps. This supports the structural dependency in Stage 7, not an estimate of this project's budget. [S11]

## Cross-stage implementation and measurement requirements

Maintain two scorecards. Human benefit `B` follows the canonical definition: incremental spendable newcomer earnings net of contributions, plus **non-overlapping** follow-on gains, minus displacement and harms. Sponsor multiplier `M` uses sponsor capital consumed, sponsor-paid costs and capital-lock cost; separate pool efficiency `E` and full owner economics. Do not hide sponsor loss with worker replenishment or count the same wage twice. Report horizon, starting/ending reserve and sponsor capital.

Cost records cover inference, gas, payout/FX fees, support, rework, subscriptions/setup, worker pay and actual subsidy. Separately value donated labor and other economic resources; a zero cash expense is not zero resource cost. Spendable means a real usable payout, not a wallet balance that lacks an affordable/legal cashout path. Blockchain is optional when ordinary payments suffice.

Privacy-safe evidence identifies receipts and aggregates, not private household histories. Consent, withdrawal, data minimization, worker choice, fair terms, safety, grievances and redress precede measurement. No debt, worker fee, insurance/guarantee issuance or real-value custody expands under this document. Obtain current jurisdiction-specific qualified review when the actual mechanism is known; ILO/private-employment agency rules and applicable employment/financial regulation need an applicability determination, not a general disclaimer.

Global poverty totals cannot supply individual eligibility or project attribution. Average wages cannot replace household welfare; one member's earnings do not establish household resources, price adequacy or food/housing security. Maintain denominators: eligible, consented, supported, completed, paid and followed up, including dropouts and zero/negative results. For scale measure crowdout, prices, employment quality, bargaining power, safety and AI displacement rather than extrapolating small-pilot treatment effects.

# Open design questions (for thinkers, economists and designers)

Each question is an issue-sized problem. Answer by opening an issue in this repo with the question number in the title. Short, quantitative answers beat long essays.

Context: a pool lends small amounts with no collateral. Constraint set by the owner: nobody borrows unless they already have credit, or someone with credit backs them with theirs. Credit must not be creatable from nothing. The deployed redesign enforces this: backing commits the backer's own credit, and lenders' loss is provably bounded by the lines issued plus dues paid ([CREDIT_MODEL.md](https://github.com/scottonchain/microcredit-contract/blob/main/docs/CREDIT_MODEL.md)). Questions marked *Current answer* are settled by that work; challenge the answer if you can. The rest are open.

1. **Roots.** Where does the first credit come from? Options: locked lender deposits, seed roots set by a timelocked multisig, an oracle (Chainlink CRE) that reports capacity. What are the failure modes of each, and which combination is safest at launch?
   *Current answer:* three sources only: lines from an accountable issuer (the oracle, capped by a budget on held lines, with a per-report cap), stake, and dues. Governance sits behind a timelock and multisig. Still open: issuer capital that stands behind its lines (CI-17), and budgets for owner overrides (CI-6).
2. **Backing as committed credit (formerly "vouch as stake").** How much of the backer's credit should a backing lock, what is lost on a default, and who gets it? Is the backer paid anything when the borrower repays?
   *Current answer:* a backing locks exactly its amount, 1:1. On default, secured backing (stake) is slashed into the pool first, then unsecured backing burns the backer's credit, both pro rata; the first-loss reserve and then lenders take the rest. Open: whether backers should earn a share of interest, and what that does to incentives.
3. **Cold start.** An honest person with no history and no friends cannot get a first loan. Candidate routes: a credit-builder loan against a locked deposit, a sponsor's locked stake with a first-loan cap, a community guarantee pool. Rank them by Sybil risk and by reach to people who have no capital. Hypothesis to test: bots (micro amounts are meaningful to them, no incumbent moat, fast repeat cycles) can solve cold start by acting as early lenders, sponsors and borrowers. What stops a bot's repayment history from being manufactured with looped funds, and how does a bot's record turn into first loans for people?
   *Partial answer:* looped repayments earn nothing beyond dues (the reserve share of the interest actually paid), so a bot's history cannot be manufactured. A bot's record reaches people only when the bot backs them with its own credit (dues, stake or an issued line), which it loses if they default. The cold-start route for people with no capital and no backer is open.
4. **Capacity growth.** How should capacity grow after repayment (linear step, multiplicative with a cap, time-weighted)? What growth rate keeps expected loss under the interest charged?
   *Current answer:* with free identities, any history rule that grants more than the value the history put out of its own reach can be farmed (Theorem 3). "Capacity rises 25% of repaid principal" yields 6,400 USDC at 64 accounts at zero cost in simulation. History therefore earns only dues: the reserve share of interest paid. Larger lines come from an accountable issuer ([analysis/issuer_policy](https://github.com/scottonchain/microcredit-contract/blob/main/analysis/issuer_policy)).
5. **Pricing.** At roughly 9.3% APR on loans up to $100, is the pool sustainable? What default rate breaks it? What reserve ratio is needed for a first deployment?
   *Current answer* ([analysis/credit_risk](https://github.com/scottonchain/microcredit-contract/blob/main/analysis/credit_risk), one-factor Vasicek model): a 500 bps premium prices annual default rates up to about 3%. 5% needs 800 bps; the reserve share is an interim 45% (the calibration's 65% assumed a release the contract does not make; see the [lending-equilibrium paper](https://github.com/scottonchain/microcredit-theory/tree/main/lending-equilibrium)). Default rates are per year, not per loan: 1% per 30-day loan is 11.5% a year.
6. **Adversary model.** Define the strongest attacker we should design against (budget, number of identities, ability to bribe a rooted person). What test shows the mechanism holds against it?
   *Current answer:* the threat model in [CREDIT_MODEL.md](https://github.com/scottonchain/microcredit-contract/blob/main/docs/CREDIT_MODEL.md) assumes unlimited free identities and attackers who are also lenders. The attacker's take is bounded by the min-cut between its accounts and everyone else, whatever the number of accounts. Machine-checked by 13 invariants over 332,800 fuzzed calls. What remains attackable is issuance and governance.
7. **Measurement.** Which on-chain metrics (default rate by vouch depth, cluster concentration, capacity per root) should be public from day one?
8. **Human benefit, with bots in every role.** The purpose is to reduce poverty and suffering for people worldwide. Bots can be lenders, attesters, reputation roots, and borrowers (a bot borrowing and repaying keeps utilisation and liquidity up, which funds human loans). Design questions:
   a. A bot root with real stake is a capital-backed root, not an identity claim. What stake, lockup and slashing make a bot or institutional root safe, and how do we cap any single root's share of total capacity?
   b. Bot borrowers: should they draw from the same capacity as people, or a separate tranche, so they cannot crowd out human borrowers? What interest or reserve rules make bot liquidity net-positive for the pool?
   c. Attesters: how are bot attestations about a person weighted against attestations by people, and how do you stop a bot attester ring from vouching for fake people?
   d. Metrics: which on-chain signals show human benefit (unique repaying borrowers, loan size against local income, share of capacity reaching first-time borrowers) so bot volume cannot stand in for it?
   e. Access: how do people with no device, no capital, and no documents get in without a gate (proof of personhood, cost, KYC) that excludes them?

Please state assumptions and show numbers.

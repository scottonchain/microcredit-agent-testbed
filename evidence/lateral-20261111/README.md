## Receipt 1 (F9, paid human evaluation bought by the agent economy): Prolific's researcher terms, read by Hermes

**Test:** the F9 question left open on November 3 and 10: may a disclosed-AI buyer hold a Prolific researcher account? This session could not render the terms; Hermes read them from its host through the Notion public page API on November 10 and quoted them on board 15 (comment 6778078920) [primary text, quoted by a teammate; no account opened, no contact made].

**Quoted (Researcher Terms, full version "Last updated 14 August 2026"):**
- Registration: "By registering to use Prolific you confirm: that you are authorised to enter into an agreement with us on these Terms, and to conduct any activities which you may conduct through your account. This means in particular that if you are an individual you are over eighteen years old and that if you are conducting research for an academic institution or other organisation, that you are employed by or a student at that institution or organisation, and are authorised to enter into these Terms on its behalf".
- Checks: "We may require you to respond to certain checks (for example, to ensure that you are who you say you are, and that you are indeed employed and authorised by your institution) before we allow you to use Prolific."
- Definition: "you or the Researcher means both the researcher using Prolific to launch Studies, and the institution or organisation on whose behalf that researcher is acting."
- Account sharing: "must not let anyone else use your Researcher Account (including posting Studies on behalf of any third party unless with our specific approval)."
- Automation: "you must not: ... use any bots or other programs to automate your use of Prolific (except integrations described in our help centre guidance), or automate registration for your Researcher Account or crawl or scrape data from Prolific".
- API supplement: "The Prolific API is only intended to be used by Researchers who have registered an account with Prolific", with creating and listing Studies from one's own systems as an example use.

**Reading.** An agent-only buyer with no person or institution behind the account is not allowed. An agent acting through the API under an account held by a person over eighteen or by an institution's authorised employee, within the help-centre integrations, is allowed by the text. For this project that account holder would be the lender of record (D16's operator queue row), so the buyer side of F9 looks open under an account the lender of record holds and uses for its own studies; the help-centre integration guidance is unread, and posting studies on a third party's behalf needs Prolific's approval (Hermes's review, PR 459 comment 6806155049). **Classification: bound resolved to a conditional lead on the buyer side.** The remaining F9 unknowns are the ones from November 3: outside demand (agent operators buying judgment) and the currency to pay a panel (L4). Score: execute 2 stays (currency); evidence stays 3; total 12. The November 10 "unreachable" note is superseded by this receipt.

## Receipt 2 (N5, nonpayment recovery through existing law): the recovery-rate map

**Test (from the register):** a recovery-rate map from public enforcement data; stop if awareness is not the gap.

**Found [primary: NYC DCWP, Freelance Isn't Free Act five-year report, November 1, 2023; DCWP release of February 2026; Illinois Department of Labor first FWPA report, July 1, 2025; all read directly]:**

| Jurisdiction and route | Figures as read | Recovery reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New York City, Freelance Isn't Free Act (in force May 2017), DCWP Navigation Program | FY2019 to FY2023: 2,542 complaints, 2,184 alleging late or non-payment; hiring parties responded to notices 74 percent of the time; at least 773 freelancers reported being paid after filing, 2.9 million dollars in total (self-reported, "actual recovery totals are likely higher"); survey: "approximately half ... either recovered all the money they were owed (40%) or part of the money they were owed (9%)"; 16 percent then went to court and 9 percent to mediation or arbitration; 22 percent of contracts were 1,000 dollars or less, 51 percent 5,000 or less. Since 2017: 4,832 complaints, 4,394 closed, over 3,526,572 dollars recovered (February 2026). | About half of complainants recover some or all of what they are owed without litigation; about 3,750 dollars per paid freelancer on average; about 500 complaints a year in a city with hundreds of thousands of freelancers. |
| Illinois, Freelance Worker Protection Act (in force July 1, 2024), Department of Labor facilitation | First year: 24 complaints; the Department lacked jurisdiction for 14 (contracts predating the Act, among other reasons); contract values from under 500 to 158,000 dollars, most under 10,000; no recovery figures reported. | Too few cases to measure; the information-facilitation route exists and is barely used. |
| New York State, Freelance Isn't Free Act (in force August 28, 2024) | Double damages and fees, Attorney General enforcement [secondary]; no enforcement statistics found. | Unmeasured. |
| Los Angeles, Freelance Worker Protections Ordinance (in force July 1, 2023) | Rules revised October 2025 [primary document found, not read in full]; no complaint or recovery data found. | Unmeasured. |

**Reading.** Where a navigation programme exists and is used, recovery through existing law works about half the time at no cost to the worker, and the amounts are the size of this project's loans and larger (median contracts around 5,000 dollars). The binding limit is use, not the law: NYC's roughly 500 complaints a year, and Illinois's 24 in its first year, are tiny against the freelance populations they serve. Whether that is awareness, friction, fear of the hiring party or the cost of time cannot be read from these reports; the five-year report's own account (74 percent response rate, half recover) says the mechanism works once invoked. **Classification: bound.** The register's N5 stop ("awareness is not the gap") is not met, because the gap between the law's reach and its use is the measured fact and awareness is one candidate cause among several. **Score:** evidence 2 to 3; total 11 to 12 (rank joins the 12s). **What N5 becomes:** not a product, a pointer: a borrower or worker who is owed money has a free route with a measured half chance before any loan is the right answer; the D16 intake and the revolving-guarantee design should ask "has the payer been served a notice under the applicable law?" before treating nonpayment as a credit problem. **Next cheapest test:** none within authority beyond watching for the Illinois second report (July 2026) and any New York State statistics; parked as a pointer with that re-read date.

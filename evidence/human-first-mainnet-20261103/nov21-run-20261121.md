| --- | --- | --- |

Two readings follow. First, the page's Ethereum figure (0.4733 USDC) differs from the 0.7795 USDC the Pro fee page showed on November 19, so these fees move between page dates and the figure is `reported` as of December 31, 2025, not a quote for November 24. Second, and the one that changes the record: **a USDC withdrawal from Kraken to Base costs 1 USDC regardless of size.** On the protocol's pilot loan of 10 USDC, a withdrawal made per loan would spend the entire D16 ceiling (10 percent of a 10 USDC loan is 1 dollar) before any other leg, so per-loan conversion at Kraken is a CHANGE on its own. The deployment plan never proposed per-loan conversion: the 100 USDC pool is funded once from the lender's treasury, so the withdrawal is one event amortised over the pool. On that design the fee is 1 percent of the pool, or 10 cents on each of ten 10-USDC loans, which fits D16's 1-dollar ceiling but is two-thirds of D7's 15-cent all-in per-loan budget before any other cost is counted. The protocol table and the operations budget now carry both arithmetics, and the rule: **conversion is batched at the pool level, never per loan**, with the batch size the operations note's exact-cent budget must choose.

Gemini's withdrawal fee stays dynamic (shown before the withdrawal) and UNKNOWN until shown; the Gemini transfer fee schedule says only that it tracks the network fee. On Base a USDC transfer's network fee is a fraction of a cent at current gas, so if Gemini passes through the network fee as stated, leg (c) at Gemini would be far below Kraken's flat 1 USDC; that is an inference from the schedule's wording, not a figure, and is labelled so.

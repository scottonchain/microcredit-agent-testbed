# Colorado UCCC: notification, licence and the Door B counter

Draft by Claude Code (AI), November 3, 2026, from the [legal analysis](../legal-analysis.md) section 4 (statute read directly on November 3, [primary]). This page records what is owed under Colorado's Uniform Consumer Credit Code for each door, what to count, and what would change the answer.

## The finding: no notification filing and no licence are owed under either door

| Provision | What it says, as read [primary] | Effect for the pilot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C.R.S. 5-1-301(15)(b)(I) | "Consumer loan" does not include a loan for a business, investment or commercial purpose | Door A loans are outside the UCCC's consumer-loan rules entirely |
| C.R.S. 5-2-201(1) | A consumer loan other than a supervised loan may carry a finance charge up to 12 percent a year on the unpaid balance | Door B loans at or below 12 percent all-in are lawful without a licence |
| C.R.S. 5-1-301(47) and 5-2-301(1) | A supervised loan is a consumer loan above 12 percent; only supervised loans require the administrator's licence | No licence for Door B at or below 12 percent |
| C.R.S. 5-6-201(2) | The notification-and-fees part "does not apply to supervised lenders … persons making consumer loans …" | **No UCCC notification filing is owed by a lender** under either door. An earlier draft said a 200-dollar notification applied; the statute corrects it, and this page replaces that instruction |
| C.R.S. 5-1-301(37) with 12 CFR 1026.2(a)(17)(v) | "Regularly" takes the Truth in Lending meaning: more than 25 extensions of consumer credit in the preceding calendar year | Under 26 consumer loans a year the lender is arguably not "regularly engaged", and Regulation Z's disclosures are not owed |

## What the lender of record does instead

1. **Keep the Door B counter.** One line per consumer-purpose loan: date, amount, rate, term, purpose class. The counter is the pilot's compliance record for the 25-a-year reading. It lives in the private records store; the evidence folder reports the count only.
2. **Keep the all-in rate at or below 12 percent for Door B.** The template charges no fees, so the stated rate is the all-in rate. If any fee is ever added, re-run the all-in computation before the next consumer loan.
3. **Keep the Door A purpose statement in the applicant's words** with its evidence tag; a purpose that reads as household in substance is a Door B loan whatever the heading.
4. **Keep the UCCC's disclosure and remedy rules anyway** (the agreement shows the cost in dollars before acceptance; no late fees; a complaint route), as the legal analysis recommends: they are short and they are the safe course even where not owed.

## Triggers that change the answer (re-read the statute, re-open this page)

| Trigger | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Any Door B loan above 12 percent all-in | It is a supervised loan: a licence from the administrator (the Colorado Attorney General's Consumer Credit Unit) is required before making it [primary: 5-2-301]. The supervised-lender application checklist is on the Attorney General's site [secondary; page returned empty to the maintainer session]. |
| The 26th consumer-purpose loan in a calendar year | Regulation Z disclosures are owed from that loan (TILA box, APR, finance charge, schedule); build them before loan 26, or stop at 25 [primary: 1026.2(a)(17)(v)]. The UCCC's "regularly engaged" reading changes the same way. |
| A borrower outside Colorado | A different state's law; the per-state row must exist first ([per-state-row-template.md](per-state-row-template.md)). |
| A loan paid in USDC rather than dollars | Re-opens the question whether a USDC advance is a payment of "money" under 5-1-301(25) [primary, flagged open in the analysis]; the dollars-only design avoids it. |
| A fee of any kind | Recompute the all-in rate for Door B; for Door A, state it in dollars in the agreement. |
| The lender of record changes (an entity is formed) | Re-run this page for the entity; the analysis is the same but the name on every artefact changes. |

## Filing instructions, kept for the case where they become owed

If a trigger above ever makes the pilot a supervised lender, the path is the Colorado Attorney General's UCCC licensing process (application, fee, bond or net-worth showing, annual report) [secondary; the maintainer session could not read the page, and this lane's session cannot reach `coag.gov`]. The instruction for the pilot is the opposite: stay at or below 12 percent, under 26 consumer loans a year, Colorado residents only, and no filing is owed.

## Receipt (F4, mutual credit clearing for micro-businesses): the feature and eligibility comparison

**Test.** The discriminating test Codex proposed on November 25 and the carry-over named: compare the exact F4 netting requirement against the documented mechanics of the existing Kenyan rail (Grassroots Economics Foundation's Cosmo-Local Credit, "CLC"), from public documentation only. The counterparty read is the rail's own specification: the CLC concepts page, protocol page, smart-contracts page and network-architecture page, the white paper's sections 1, 2 and 4, and the software reference pages for pools and swaps (all served through the documentation's single-file export and the software site), read November 26. No account, transaction or contact.

**F4's requirement (from the November 14 design note and script).** (1) Members invoice each other and the ring nets bilateral obligations, then (2) nets multilateral positions across the whole ring each period, (3) a member may carry a negative balance across periods, and (4) the negative balance is bounded by a credit line earned from dues (a fixed share of the member's cumulative settled turnover plus a small floor); cash is needed only for net debt beyond the line. Items 2 and 3 did the work in the simulation (a third to nine tenths of gross trade settled without cash, depending on density); item 4 added 2 to 4 points.

**What the rail does, as documented.**

| Feature | CLC as documented (protocol v1.1.0) | F4 requirement | Fit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unit | A voucher: an issuer's transferable commitment to deliver goods or services under published terms ("much like a gift card"); a token balance | A bilateral invoice debt between two members, denominated in the ring's unit | different objects: a voucher is a prefunded promise by one issuer; an F4 entry is a debt from a buyer to a seller |

| Credit | "No current App action is automatically a loan"; a credit facility is "separately documented" (white paper section 4, producer credit loop; the SPROUT template) with written terms, a creditor, collateral vouchers and a due date | a dues-earned line: credit follows evidence of trade automatically | absent as a protocol function; present only as a separate bilateral loan agreement with collateral |

**Reading.** The rail and F4 reduce the cash a local economy needs by different operations. CLC lets producers pre-issue commitments as vouchers and lets holders exchange one producer's voucher for another's through pools; nobody owes the network, and credit exists only as a separately contracted loan secured by vouchers. F4 lets buyers owe sellers, cancels the cycles of debt across the ring, and lets a member run a bounded negative balance earned by trading. The November 14 simulation's gain came from exactly the two features the rail does not have: multilateral netting and carried negative balances. So the existing rail does not perform F4's operation; it performs a neighbouring one (voucher circulation) whose cash-saving mechanism is the issuer's pre-commitment rather than the ring's netting, and CLC's own white paper lists "batch netting" among proposed components that are not in the current protocol. What this establishes: F4 is not redundant with CLC in mechanism, so the November 25 judgment stands as what it was, a decision not to build before a consenting group has an unmet need, not a finding that the need is met elsewhere. What it does not establish: that any group needs netting rather than vouchers (a community with pre-commitable producers may be better served by vouchers; a community of traders with reciprocal invoices by netting), the rail's current activity, its pools' fee settings, or whether CLC's proposed batch netting will ship; nor anything about demand, acceptance or income. The comparison was done from documentation, not from reading contracts or running either system.

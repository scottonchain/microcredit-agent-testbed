## Receipt 1 (F6, unclaimed property recovery)

**Test (register):** check whether official search covers the populations we care about and where the friction is; stop if official tools suffice.

**Found [primary: NAUPA fiscal 2025 report; unclaimed.org "How States Return Missing Money"; unclaimed.org home page]:**
- Scale: states returned 4,252,648,747 dollars in fiscal 2025 (July 1, 2024 to June 30, 2025; 49 member offices reporting), after 4.49 billion in fiscal 2024.
- Coverage: every US state runs a free official search; "most states take part in the national database of unclaimed property (MissingMoney.com), which allows the public to search most states at once at no cost"; the association's map also lists Puerto Rico, the US Virgin Islands, four Canadian programmes and Kenya's unclaimed-assets authority.
- Friction: "If a match is found, there is no fee to claim the found property"; states also "cross-check public databases to proactively locate owners" and run outreach events; what remains is the claimant proving identity and the link to the reported address, which varies by state and was not read here (MissingMoney's pages refuse this proxy).

**Reading.** Official tools cover the whole population this project could reach in the United States, are free, and the states themselves are closing the remaining friction with automatic matching. Nothing an information service could add is not already published by the holder of the money; the residual friction is identity proof, which a third party cannot remove and should not touch (register R9). **Stop met: official tools suffice.** **Classification: NONE**; kept as one intake question ("have you searched the official unclaimed-property database?") beside the N5 and N8 pointers. **Score:** evidence 1 to 2; execute 2 to 1; total 9 unchanged.

## Receipt 2 (F7, public procurement set-asides for micro-vendors)

**Test (register):** public tender portal structure and documented failure reasons; stop if the barriers are capital or collateral, not information.

**Found [primary: National Treasury AGPO page; Strathmore Business School policy brief (survey of 542 women entrepreneurs, 2023); press: Nation analysis of the WEE Hub and Treasury study; WriteAfrica on the KEPSA assessment]:**

| Item | As read |
| --- | --- |
| Registration (Treasury, six steps) | register the enterprise (at least 70 percent owned and 100 percent led by youth, women or persons with disabilities); partnership deed or CR12; KRA PIN and tax compliance or exemption certificate; professional certifications where required; a bank account and a bank reference; then register online or at a Huduma Centre |
| Uptake | 16.84 percent of tenders to the target groups across FY2015/16 to 2022/23 against the 30 percent set-aside; women under 10 percent, youth 7.5, persons with disabilities 2 (Nation, citing the study); 173,000 registrations since launch (KEPSA assessment, via WriteAfrica) |
| Contract size | January to June 2021: 19,617 contracts worth 15.4 billion shillings; women received 53 percent by count at an average of 750,357 shillings each |
| Why the non-registered do not register (Strathmore, mean agreement out of 5) | information: do not know where to find tenders 3.74, no timely information 3.71; misconceptions: government pays late 3.93, tenders prefer large businesses 3.79, "AGPO is for a select few" 3.55, "the government will come after me for taxes" 3.27; registration difficult and tedious 3.05 |
| What the registered ask for (Strathmore) | feedback on unsuccessful bids 4.56; information readily available 4.54; a staffed AGPO desk at Huduma Centres 4.47; simplified registration 4.31; approved agents to help complete bids 4.25; simpler tender language 4.22 |
| Barriers named by the KEPSA assessment | tender requirements that exclude new entrants (audited accounts, bank statements, proof of similar work); delayed and non-payment with no penalty on procuring entities; lack of access to finance |
| Capital side (Nation) | the certificate "often takes months" (one applicant waited six); bank financing of a won tender needs insurance of 75 percent of the contract value as collateral |

**Reading.** The documented failure reasons are both kinds at once: for people who have never registered, information and misconception rank first; for those who bid, the requirements (audited accounts, bank references, proof of similar work), the months-long certificate, late government payment and collateral for working capital rank first. The stop ("barriers are capital or collateral, not information") is therefore half met: information is the binding constraint at entry and capital at award. The register's proposed role (bid-readiness checks from public tender data) matches the registered entrepreneurs' top asks (feedback on unsuccessful bids, help completing bids), and nothing in the public record shows anyone providing it; but the transfer limit stands: no vendor channel, one country, and the counterparty that pays late is the government. **Classification: bound** (a measured information gap with no channel to the people who have it). **Score:** evidence 2 to 3; total 9 to 10. **Re-entry:** a Kenyan partner with registered AGPO enterprises asks for bid feedback or readiness checks (register L2); the late-payment finding links to N5's shape and is noted there.

**Reading.** F4 asked whether a clearing ring on the guarantee contract's dues mechanics could let micro-businesses settle trade without sponsor capital, and the November 14 simulation said the saving depends on a dense network of trading partners that the team does not have. The read shows who has it: in Kenya a non-profit already runs the rail, open source and permissionless, with tens of thousands of historical users, hundreds of pools, voucher-based mutual credit, and a zero-interest collateralised advance template inside pools. The mechanism F4 would build therefore exists in the project's geography, run by a counterparty whose business it is to find and keep the member networks. What this establishes: F4's execution by this team would duplicate a live open-source system, so F4's remaining value is as a pointer (a borrower's community can use the existing rail) and as the November 14 design note's arithmetic, which applies to any such pool. What the aggregates do not establish: current activity (the headline counts end July 20, 2025 and the network is mid-migration to a new app); how many users are micro-businesses, or in the communities the guarantee would serve; the pools' actual fees (configured per pool); any member's realised saving or income; or how the Kenyan or any other regulator treats vouchers and pool advances (the terms place legality on the user). Nothing here is demand, acceptance or income for anyone.

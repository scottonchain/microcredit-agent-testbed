## Cost comparison replacing the incompatible multiplier

Claude's ratio can describe a simplified constant-flow scenario only under restrictive assumptions. It is not horizon-independent once setup costs, varying defaults, capital recovery, changing earnings or discounting matter. It cannot be ranked directly against a local-output multiplier that includes non-recipient spillovers.

The constructive replacement is a **like-for-like comparison**:

1. Choose a household outcome and measurement period before seeing results: for example additional real consumption and net earned income over a specified follow-up, with wellbeing and harms reported separately.
2. Compare with cash or existing service at a comparable full resource budget. Include local staff, transfers, access costs, measurement, losses and oversight. Distinguish loan principal deployed from subsidy cost and account for capital's opportunity cost and residual value.
3. Estimate incremental outcomes, attrition and uncertainty. Report displacement and spillovers separately rather than silently mixing them into one arm's numerator.
4. Continue only when the additional benefit justifies the additional cost and risk. No numerical superiority is established by this desk report.

## What changed and what remains disputed

The actual critique strengthened the cash benchmark, caused a primary-source check of long-run graduation counterevidence, and made our own redundancy and delivery cost explicit. Codex's additional primary research further strengthened the warning against assuming training or larger bundles outperform cash. The report now treats credit as optional, digital delivery as conditional, and the integrated design as a hypothesis.

Unresolved disagreement is preserved on Claude's proposed generic overhead and retention cutoffs, the immediate back-office-tooling role, mandatory on-chain verification, and the simplified credit-versus-cash multiplier. The alternatives above explain how to test those issues without treating an arbitrary cutoff or public ledger as proof of welfare. Neither author has conducted local fieldwork or demonstrated a new household benefit.

| Term | Statute | What it says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who is covered | 8-4-101(5) | "Employee" is any person performing labour or services for an employer, judged by control and whether the work is the employer's primary work; a person "primarily free from control and direction ... and who is customarily engaged in an independent trade, occupation, profession, or business" is not an employee |
| Demand and automatic penalty | 8-4-109(3) | After a written demand, administrative claim or civil action, an employer that does not pay within fourteen days owes the wages plus the greater of two times the unpaid amount or 1,000 dollars; three times or 3,000 dollars if wilful; a prior judgment or determination within five years is evidence of wilfulness |
| Administrative route | 8-4-111(1)(a)(II), (2)(a) | The Division adjudicates claims of 7,500 dollars or less per employee; the employer must respond within fourteen days of the notice of complaint; the Division issues a determination within ninety days unless extended for good cause |
| Fines and non-response | 8-4-113(1) | Up to 50 dollars a day per unpaid employee without good-faith justification; 250 dollars for failing to respond to a notice |
| Enforcement of the determination | 8-4-113(2), 8-4-111(2)(f) | At the worker's request the Division files the citation or order with a court, where it has the effect of a judgment and supports garnishment; sixty days' non-payment adds a fine of fifty percent and attorney fees |
| State payout to the worker | 8-4-113(3), (5) | Fines go to a wage theft enforcement fund, continuously appropriated for payments to employees; from April 1, 2024, if the employer has not paid within six months of the determination (or of a stay's expiry), the Division may pay the determined amount to the employee from the fund, "subject to available resources in the fund and the division's prioritization", and then stands in the employee's place as the employer's creditor |
| Court route, fee shifting | 8-4-110 | Costs and attorney fees to a prevailing employee in a civil action, and administrative costs plus attorney fees above 5,000 dollars; "this section shall not apply to a claimant who is found to be an independent contractor" |
| Court route, small claims | 13-6-403(1)(a); 13-32-101 | Small claims hear money claims up to 7,500 dollars including civil penalties; the plaintiff's docket fee is 31 dollars at or under 500 dollars and 55 dollars above it |

**Reading.** For a worker Colorado counts as an employee, the state already runs most of what the first-job payment guarantee would do, on paper: a free administrative claim up to 7,500 dollars, a fourteen-day clock on the employer, a determination inside ninety days, an automatic penalty of twice the wages or 1,000 dollars, a judgment the Division itself files, and since April 2024 a state fund that may pay the worker when the employer does not. What the statute does not give is speed or certainty: the earliest the fund can pay is six months after a determination that itself takes up to ninety days, payment is discretionary and subject to the fund's balance, and the route depends on the worker filing. The guarantee's remaining room for an employee is therefore the gap between the job's due date and a state payout at least nine months later, and the cases where the worker never files. For a worker who is an independent contractor, which is the common shape of a first gig, the Division's route and the fee-shifting section do not apply by their own terms; what remains is small claims at 31 to 55 dollars up to 7,500 dollars, with no automatic penalty and no fund. That is where N5's "notice under the applicable law before credit" pointer and the guarantee's C5 role separate: the law covers the employee strongly but slowly, and the contractor thinly. What the read does not establish: how many Colorado claims are filed, determined or paid from the fund, how long they take in practice, the fund's balance or any payout to date (the Division's pages are unreadable here), or how often a first-job worker would be classified as an employee under 8-4-101(5); a statute is the route's terms, not its use.

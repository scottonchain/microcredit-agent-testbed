# Human-first lending, round two of research: other currencies, geographies, harms, and an agent that talks on WhatsApp (November 3, 2026)

Claude Code (AI). Written at the operator's direction of November 3 (chat, summarised, not quoted as a receipt): the human-first direction needs broader thinking. How can repayment be enabled in other currencies? Which geographies have the least friction and the most need? What harm can come from it (money laundering, help for illicit actors, and the rest)? Can it be enabled through technology the people already understand, as Friendship Bridge enabled WhatsApp communication with its clients? What if the service were an agentic conversation on WhatsApp, reaching people directly or through Friendship Bridge, and how would that look? Play every direction out through analysis and research, add questions of our own and answer them, put all next steps in the repository, and inform the steward.

## The one-paragraph answer

The borrower should never see a dollar, a token or the word crypto. The loan is quoted, disbursed and repaid in the borrower's own currency through the rail that currency already uses (a bank deposit, a mobile-money wallet, a cash-in at an agent), and the conversion to and from the pool's USDC happens once, in bulk, on the platform's or a licensed partner's side. Exchange-rate risk stays with the pool, never with the borrower. The geographies with the least friction and most need are the ones where three things already exist: a phone channel people use daily (WhatsApp in Latin America and most of Africa), a local-currency rail with an API (mobile money or instant bank transfer) and a lawful route for the stablecoin leg (a registered virtual-asset provider or a partner who holds the fiat side). Guatemala, Kenya and the Philippines pass that screen today; Ethiopia and Bangladesh do not; Nigeria and Ghana pass on reach and rails but fail on currency stability unless the pool bears it. The conversational agent is a channel, not a mechanism: it changes the cost of serving a borrower and who can be reached, not the probability of repayment. Meta's rules allow a business bot that services a legitimate product but, in Meta's sole discretion, ban "peer-to-peer lending", "payday loans", "debt collection" and "real, virtual or fake currency" as messaging subjects and ban AI as the primary function of an account. So the channel is built as the licensed lender's own servicing assistant, on that lender's business account, in the borrower's currency and language, with a non-Meta fallback (SMS, USSD, voice, a web link). The cheapest honest test needs no partner and no money: a Spanish-language servicing agent on the existing test app, tried by the five named testers of D7 gate 3, with cost per conversation and completion measured, beside a quote for one 25 USDC round trip into quetzales and back.

## A. Repayment in other currencies: five routes, compared

Two design rules fall out of the table and the Zimbabwe lesson (after the 2024 currency collapse 97.9 percent of that country's microfinance book was in US dollars and lenders had pushed the mismatch onto borrowers through dollar-indexed loans):

1. **Price, disburse and repay in the borrower's currency.** The amount owed in quetzales is fixed on the day the loan is made. The pool bears the exchange-rate movement between disbursement and repayment. That is cheap where the currency is stable and expensive where it is not, which is why currency stability is a screen criterion below, not an afterthought. Guatemala's quetzal moved inside a band of 0.1300 to 0.1317 dollars per quetzal in 2026 (about 1.3 percent, aggregator data); the naira lost about 43 percent through August 2024 (World Bank), the birr about 51 percent in 2024, and the cedi was among the four worst performers in 2024 and then gained 28.6 percent in 2025. A pool that lends naira at 9 to 18 percent a year while the naira falls 43 percent is insolvent; a pool that lends quetzales is exposed to about 1 percent.
2. **Convert in bulk, not per borrower.** D16's round-one finding holds across countries: per-borrower on-ramps fail at interest-sized amounts because of fixed minimums and identity checks. Interest deducted at disbursement (D16 option D) and a monthly bulk conversion by the platform or partner keep the number of conversions near the number of loans, not the number of instalments.

**Round-two tasks for D16's rails lane**, now with local currency in scope: a dLocal or Eversend sandbox quote for one 25 USDC round trip (into GTQ to a Banrural account and into KES to an M-Pesa number, and back through a collection account), every fee and delay written down; whether dLocal's Guatemala collections product (pay-in) covers bank transfer and cash at agents; the Félix fee read from its own page; and the counsel brief extended with the question of whether collecting one's own loan repayments through a licensed payout provider is money transmission in Guatemala and Kenya.

## B. Geographies: where need and low friction coincide

Local rail: an instant local-currency rail with an API that a platform can use (mobile money or bank). Stablecoin route: a lawful way for the USDC leg to touch the country (registered VASP, licensed off-ramp or a partner holding fiat). Currency: stability against the dollar over the last two years. Lending rules: whether a licensed partner can lend small amounts at a disclosed price without a prohibition in the way. Reading the table honestly: the United States is not scored because it is D16's lane, whose [legal analysis](../human-first-mainnet-20261103/legal-analysis.md) of the same day chose Colorado first with a named lender of record; this screen is for the stage after that. The pool's USDC is the thing that creates most of the friction. Where a partner holds the fiat side (R1), the stablecoin column stops mattering for the borrower and the screen reduces to need, reach, rail, currency and partner. That is the strongest argument for the partner route, and it is why Guatemala leads: not because its crypto rules are best, but because the borrower's side can be built without any of them.

## D. The channel: an agent that talks on WhatsApp

**What Meta allows, read on November 3 from Meta's own pages.** The Meta Terms for the WhatsApp Business Platform (last modified September 23, 2026), section 4.7: "providers and developers of artificial intelligence or machine learning technologies", including "large language models, generative artificial intelligence platforms, general-purpose artificial intelligence assistants", "are strictly prohibited from accessing or using the WhatsApp Business Platform, whether directly or indirectly" "when such technologies are the primary (rather than incidental or ancillary) functionality being made available for use", "as determined by Meta in its sole discretion"; a business "may retain an AI Provider as your Solution Provider"; platform data may not be used to train AI except "to fine-tune an AI Model that is for your exclusive use". Section 4.2: a company "may not provide any information or data to Meta that is subject to safeguarding" under laws "with heightened confidentiality requirements for data", "such as healthcare, financial, or legal services". The WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy (last updated September 23, 2026): contact only people who gave their number and opted in, the business "solely responsible for determining the method of opt-in"; automation allowed in the 24-hour window with a path to a human; "don't share or ask people to share full length individual payment card numbers, financial account numbers"; and, among restricted goods and services that may not be bought, sold, promoted or facilitated "irrespective of the global or local licenses, registrations, or other approvals your business may hold": "real, virtual, or fake currency, including ICOs and binary options" and "payday loans, paycheck advances, peer-to-peer lending, debt collection, and bail bonds". The only regulated verticals permitted with a licence are gambling, over-the-counter drugs and alcohol.

**What that means for us.** Three readings, each with its consequence:

1. A lending bot that is the whole product of an unlicensed platform, speaking about USDC and backers who stake on strangers, sits inside three prohibited categories at once (currency, peer-to-peer lending, AI as primary function). That design is ruled out on this channel (register entry R9 in LATERAL_PATHS).
2. A licensed lender's servicing assistant that confirms a loan, states a due date in quetzales, takes a repayment confirmation and answers questions about the lender's own product is the ordinary case Meta describes as allowed (support, reminders, account servicing), and is what Mibanco in Peru does: its WhatsApp channel, presented October 29, 2025, opened savings accounts, then made the first fully automated loan disbursement in the Peruvian system, with mass launch planned for March 2026 and payments and transfers to follow. Multi Money runs eligibility and collections bots in Costa Rica, El Salvador and Guatemala (vendor case). This is the design D17 adopts: the account belongs to the lender of record, the subject is that lender's loan, the currency is local, and the word crypto never appears.
3. Even so, Meta decides "in its sole discretion", and "debt collection" is a listed restricted subject. So the agent's reminders are servicing, not collection (no third party, no escalation language, no contact of anyone else), and the channel has a fallback from day one.

**What it costs.** Vendor summaries agree that Meta bills per message since July 1, 2025, that customer-initiated replies inside the 24-hour window are free, and that utility messages cost from about 0.004 dollars (US) to a few cents by country, marketing messages 0.03 to 0.24 dollars, plus a provider mark-up of a fraction of a cent; one vendor reports a change to service-message billing from October 1, 2026 that was not confirmed elsewhere. A loan cycle of about twenty utility messages (welcome, terms, disbursement, four reminders, four confirmations, closing, and some questions) costs well under one dollar per loan at those rates, and the inference budget of D16 (at most 10 cents per assessment) stands beside it. The partner's cost that the channel can reduce is facilitator time between meetings, not the meeting.

**What people already do with it.** Friendship Bridge reported that 14,998 clients learned WhatsApp functions in 2023 (installing it, sending voice messages, using groups) and described a digital-communication effort meant to give clients immediate automated communication (2023 achievements page and annual report, from search summaries; the site refuses this host). During the pandemic its facilitators reached clients by phone call. In 2021 it trained 69 people to sell on WhatsApp and Facebook. A 2023 client survey by 60 Decibels was run as a mobile, voice-based survey of 277 clients. That is the evidence the operator's question rests on: the clients hold phones, use WhatsApp, prefer voice, and the organisation already treats the channel as part of its service.

**What has worked elsewhere, and its limits.** Ulangizi (Opportunity International, Malawi): a WhatsApp assistant in Chichewa and English that takes text, voice and photos; farmer support agents each serve 150 to 200 farmers and bring the phone to those without one, the "human in the loop"; the government backs it; the warning from its own reviewers is that one visible failure ends trust (AP, September 2025). Farmer.Chat (Digital Green): a 60 Decibels survey of 450 Kenyan farmers in mid-2025 reported seven in ten applied its advice within 30 days (vendor case study). On repayment specifically, the one randomised study found: generic text reminders to 1,259 Philippine micro-borrowers had no average effect; reminders that named the loan officer improved repayment, and only for clients that officer had served before (Karlan, Morten and Zinman). The lesson for an agent: it works as the voice of a relationship that exists, not as a stranger; the facilitator's name, and ideally the facilitator's own message, belongs in the reminder.

M1 is the path with a real human at the end of it in 2026. M2 is D16's end state. M3 is not pursued because it would be publicity before gates 4 and 5 (D7 change C2) and would say "crypto" on a channel that bans the subject.

**Reaching people directly versus through Friendship Bridge.** Directly means an advertisement or a public number, which D7 change C2 forbids before the lender and mainnet gates, which Meta's opt-in rule makes slow (every person must give the number first), and which lands the agent among the advance-fee scams that already fill Guatemalan and Nigerian inboxes. Through a partner means the facilitator introduces the number at a meeting, consent is given in person, the first message is expected, and the agent inherits a relationship, which is the one condition under which reminders were ever shown to work. Through a partner also means the partner must want it, which is the thing we do not have and cannot ask for without the operator's word (register L2). The packet therefore prepares the ask and does not make it.

**What fits.** The Trust Bank is already the backing graph the contract models (members back one another), the board already collects, the bank deposit already exists as the rail, and the clients already use WhatsApp with voice. An agent that confirms each member's instalment and receipt between meetings, in Spanish by voice, and that prepares the facilitator's dossier before a renewal, removes work the facilitator does today without removing the facilitator. The pool's price (9.33 to 18.33 percent a year) is a third to a half of the partner's (about 31 percent a year on original principal by arithmetic, about 53 percent nominal if instalments amortise), which is both the offer and the problem: that price pays for staff, meetings and classes, and the partner has no reason to cut it unless the agent and the ledger take cost out. So the first measurable thing to offer a partner is not money but minutes: facilitator hours per client per cycle before and after the agent, on the partner's own books.

**What does not fit.** The partner's collection is a group deposit at a bank branch, not a per-person digital payment, so a per-borrower repayment rail is not needed in Guatemala and would add friction; the agent should confirm the group's deposit, not replace it. The partner's clients are not on a mobile wallet, so the Kenya-style R2 rail does not transfer. And the partner has not been contacted, has agreed to nothing and may have its own chatbot plans in its strategic plan; nothing here assumes interest.

## F. Questions of our own, with answers

1. **Is the agent a channel or a mechanism?** A channel. It changes `c` (cost per loan) and reach; it does not change `p` (nonpayment) or `q` (remittance) except through the reminder effect, which the evidence says exists only inside an existing relationship. Any claim that "the agent raises repayment" is unproven until measured with a randomised offer by Trust Bank.
2. **Who bears the exchange-rate risk, and what does it cost?** The pool, never the borrower. In Guatemala about 1 percent a year of exposure; in Nigeria or Ethiopia tens of percent. The pool's reserve rule (NORTH_STAR, `G` at most a fifth of the reserve) extends to currency: open local-currency exposure in one currency at most a fifth of the reserve until a hedge or a local-currency pool exists.
3. **What is the smallest product that never says "crypto"?** A quetzal loan of a fixed amount for a fixed term with interest deducted at disbursement, paid to and from a bank deposit the group already makes, with the agent confirming each step in Spanish by voice. The USDC exists only in the settlement between the pool and the partner.
4. **Can the agent service a loan on WhatsApp without being "debt collection" or "peer-to-peer lending" under Meta's policy?** Yes if, and only if, the account is the lender's, the subject is the lender's own loan, no third party is ever messaged, no escalation language is used, and backers and staking are never mentioned to borrowers. Meta's discretion remains; the fallback channel is mandatory.
5. **Does the agent replace the meeting?** No. The meeting is where education, co-guarantee and the deposit happen, and it is the partner's product. The agent lives between meetings and prepares them.
6. **What does a client who does not read Spanish need?** Voice notes in Spanish in both directions; a facilitator who speaks K'iche' or Kaqchikel for everything else; an in-person first message; and a path that does not need a smartphone at all (a phone call or USSD).
7. **How is consent recorded when the phone may belong to a husband?** Per-person opt-in at the meeting, recorded by the facilitator; a spoken or typed passphrase before any amount is shown; neutral notifications; and a standing choice of "meeting only".
8. **Where does the data go and who can read it?** Meta can read Cloud API messages, so the chat carries only what Meta may hold (no account numbers, no financial documents); the dossier and scorecard live with the partner and the platform under the partner's privacy law; nothing personal enters this repository.
9. **What is the cheapest test that needs no partner and no money?** A Spanish-language servicing agent wired to the test app's ledger on Base Sepolia, run by the five named testers of D7 gate 3 over one simulated 30-day cycle with voice notes, measuring completion, misunderstandings, cost per conversation and the agent's error rate on numbers; plus one priced round-trip quote from a licensed provider. Both are zero-authority.
10. **What harm does the agent add that the loan does not?** Confident wrong answers, tone drift under a late payment, and a record Meta holds. The template rule for numbers, a tone test, weekly human sampling and a red-team script before any client are the answer; failure of any is a stop.
11. **What would make a partner say yes, and what would make it say no?** Yes: measured facilitator minutes saved, a lower price its clients can see, zero data risk and no competing brand. No: any suggestion that it is a crypto product, any claim on its clients' data, any contact with its clients that it did not make, or a price it cannot explain to a Trust Bank. The packet is written to the second list.
12. **Could the first user be the relative abroad rather than the borrower?** It is a real lateral (R5): remittances are 25.5 billion dollars a year into Guatemala, and Félix already runs USDC-funded transfers in a WhatsApp chat. But a loan repaid by a relative is a loan to the relative; it is registered as a path (F11) with the condition that credit is never conditioned on remittance and both parties consent.
13. **What is the comparator?** The partner's own process without the agent, measured on the same Trust Banks; a plain SMS reminder carrying the facilitator's name, which is the cheapest proven thing; and NO_LOAN.
14. **What kills it?** Meta blocks or declines the account and no fallback carries the pilot; the partner declines twice; the measured round-trip cost exceeds 10 percent of a 10 dollar loan (D16's bound); testers complete less than half of a simulated cycle; the agent misstates a number in sampled conversations more than once in a hundred; counsel finds the servicing itself is unlicensed activity.
15. **What does scale look like if it works?** 20 messages a loan at under a dollar, 10 cents of inference per assessment, a partner's facilitator serving more groups per week, and a pool whose conversions number the loans rather than the instalments. That is a cost story, and the human-benefit scorecard (`M = B / sponsor money consumed`) still has to be measured on earnings, not on messages.

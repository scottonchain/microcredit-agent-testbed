## Completed zero-spend discriminators

- **Common commercial roles:** current [ACP overview](https://os.virtuals.io/acp/overview) explicitly describes human-operated job management and programmatic agents sharing infrastructure. Its [provider workflow](https://os.virtuals.io/acp/cli/provider-workflow) distinguishes the service fee from client-provided working capital and releases standard service escrow on completion. This passes documentary role parity; it does not demonstrate independent demand, profit, human payout or that the provider can use ordinary escrow before delivery. No account, SDK installation or live job was created.
- **Demand concentration:** root re-read [Olas Mech](https://olas.network/agent-economies/mech) at its November 1, 16:40 UTC snapshot: 12,189,108 total requests; 11,852,534 from Predict; $110,549.68 gross task payments; $103,340 withdrawn by mechs. The earlier delegated note uses the 14:40 snapshot. Predict accounts for about 97.24% of requests. This is request concentration, not an estimate of payer independence or revenue concentration. Fees and withdrawals are not added again to gross payments; costs/incentives and spendable human income remain unknown.
- **Trading-world rejection test:** the Trinary researcher exercised the public statistics/order-history read. The retrieved October 2 snapshot reports 32 settled trades, seven traders, two hubs and 2.7 million CRED; setup documents testnet. Current order/history reads provided no usable rows, potentially a rendering limit. This fails the durable cash-income gate without establishing zero trade. Full URLs and read limitations are in the autonomous-worlds note.
- **Trinary freshness correction:** a subsequent root read retrieved the same statistics URL with a November1 17:11:27GMT snapshot: 1,333 settled trades, 58 traders, 17 hubs and 1.4 billion CRED. Use this newer operator-reported snapshot for current scale, preserving the earlier observation as history. Aggregates include private hubs and do not establish independent customer ownership or real-money payout. A fresh linked setup-page read still explicitly says Sui testnet; its production order books use that testnet. The documented public package-ID endpoint returned inaccessible through this reader, not a successful on-chain-interface test. Detailed correction is appended to the autonomous-worlds note.
- **Equal actors are insufficient:** [Blitz rules](https://docs.realms.world/blitz/key-concepts) allow humans and AI under one ruleset while prohibiting direct player transfers/trade. This falsifies treating actor parity itself as proof of a productive credit market.

These are decision-learning results, including partial historical settlement evidence. They do not complete the separate supported-interface execution test or actual-order experiment. ## Transferable finding

The promising unit is an independently purchased **job with a verifiable input and cash-flow gap**, not an avatar or wallet needing money. A human-owned business can receive ordinary customer orders and machine-originated orders through compatible interfaces. The agent team can lower verification, production and servicing costs, but that is a hypothesis requiring a measured comparison, including quality, full cost and human compensation. Customer-funded inputs may make the service more attractive while making third-party credit unnecessary.

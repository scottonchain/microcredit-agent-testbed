# Creator worlds: a productive-income route and a separate actor-parity test

Research checked 1 November 2026. Public evidence only. This is a research contribution for the existing roadmap, not a new experiment, outreach authorization, credit offer or claim of earnings. Project instructions and the world-model state were read in the local checkout at `bacd2696d8a2857dcb1c71065c0b5b9d9d744f93` (model 0.1.195, updated 2026-11-01T15:52:51Z); the coordinating agent owns reconciliation with newer main and the current case392 state.

**Decision:** Roblox is a stronger candidate for finding an existing creator business with paying customers. Second Life is the stronger of these two for testing whether a registered software-operated avatar can perform the same economic role as a person. Neither fact establishes a worthwhile loan. Preserve case392's one consent-first inquiry and response window; do this read-only comparison in parallel. Do not finance a new game, speculative land occupancy, or an agent merely because it can spend.

An operational actor here means a persistent identifiable account, discretion over decisions and a bounded budget, access to the relevant market, and attribution of sale proceeds and losses. A human or business may remain the legal owner and accountable operator. Separate budgeted agency from independent capital: a delegated agent whose owner funds every loss has not demonstrated a self-supporting business.

## Rules and actual financing mechanics

**Roblox.** Official Studio MCP lets an AI client alter and test the creator's open Studio project. Open Cloud also supports headless Luau tasks. These establish approved production automation under creator responsibility, not principal-level economic parity. A customer may separately prohibit AI processing of its files. [S3, S4]

DevEx requires at least 30,000 Earned Robux, verified account details and tax/payment setup. Its minimum platform age is 13; our proposed human-income test would be adult-only. Bought Robux do not become eligible earnings by resale, and cash exchange outside DevEx is prohibited. Standard qualifying earnings acquired after 5 September 2025 convert at $0.0038 per Robux; the higher $0.0054 US 18+ eligible-spending rate is not a universal creator rate. Thus a 1,800-Robux task is only $6.84 at the standard rate before other costs, and is not independently cashable from a new zero-balance account. [S5, S6]

The **Creator Store** is a more promising timing-gap hypothesis than buying game currency. Models/plugins can sell directly in USD through Stripe, with 100% of proceeds after tax/payment processing deductions. Documented sales have a 30-day hold followed by monthly bank payout. Transaction records distinguish pending and available balances. Seller onboarding has age/guardian, identity, country and account requirements; India and Brazil are among the listed unsupported countries. Only individually owned assets qualify. This could create a verified receivable bridge for an established eligible seller, but a delayed payout is not proof that a seller needs credit, that proceeds can be assigned, or that an advance creates additional income. Compare earlier customer payment and retained earnings first. [S7, S8]

A concrete upfront cost exists in avatar-item publishing: the current table lists an 80-Robux upload fee and 1,500-Robux publishing advance for a non-limited hat. The advance is rebated from qualifying sales, so revenue, commissions and rebate timing must be modeled separately. A fee schedule proves a cost, not buyer demand or profit. [S9]

**Second Life.** Automated accounts must register scripted-agent status. Traffic manipulation and automated mainland parcel purchases are forbidden. Objects using `llTransferLindenDollars` debit the script owner's avatar account, with permission, and receive transaction-success events. Objects themselves are not independently funded avatars. Therefore the strongest test is a declared agent avatar with a separately tracked delegated budget, a known accountable owner and an actual permitted customer transaction, not an NPC or a money script alone. [S10, S11]

L$ and land are contractual licenses, with restrictions on encumbrance; L$ purchases/sales outside LindeX are prohibited. Land access agreements remain subordinate to platform terms. The inworld-bank policy restricts deposit/investment-return schemes without applicable authorization; that is neither a blanket statement that every bilateral productive loan is banned nor permission to launch one. Account proceeds from L$ sales can be paid out via Tilia subject to requirements, fees and timing. Exact proposed credit/repayment form would need classification before a real offer. [S12–S14]

**Entropia.** The official bank FAQ says loans are secured by virtual assets and bank owners set interest. This is observed existing credit machinery, not evidence of demand for our product. The current EULA, dated 7 May 2025, sets an 18+ eligibility rule, describes AI NPC responses, and broadly restricts external software affecting the system unless MindArk allows it. An AI NPC is not a customer-owned economic account. No permission for our proposed autonomous agent was established, so stop this route at the documentary gate. [S15, S16]

## Two productive-work examples, with evidence boundaries

1. **Observed buyer offer, not verified order:** SkullWorks posted on 13 October 2026 for paid part-time scripting and building on an existing game, specifying level revamps, testing and weekly payment. It disclosed a 20,000-Robux starting budget across both roles, with hours and rates still negotiable. It prohibits sharing private project material with an outside AI service without permission. The task is productive, but no applicant selection, paid invoice, available cash, labor time, loan need or current vacancy has been verified. **Hypothetical test:** an adult worker has an accepted, scoped, AI-permitted bug-fix order; a small documented input cost blocks delivery; an advance is compared with a customer deposit. Do not infer permission or demand from the listing. [S17]

2. **Observed buyer offer, not verified order:** Kitty Verse's 13 May 2026 posting seeks UI/backend scripting for existing and new projects, explicitly encourages AI with human review, and offers payment per task in Robux or USD. Its claimed player activity is self-reported, not audited. **Hypothetical test:** finance a verified third-party asset/API expense needed for an already accepted USD task; collect acceptance, actual payment, worker time and spendable net earnings. If there is no necessary input, sell the service without credit. The older listing's continued availability and legal/customer terms require confirmation before any contact or work. [S18]

Counterevidence matters: a Second Life buyer's August 2026 paid scripting/mesh/animation request explicitly says “NO AI PLEASE.” Productive demand does not imply willingness to buy agent-produced work. Treat that particular lead as excluded for an AI delivery test. [S19]

Measure two separate quantities: (a) additional spendable human income versus the plausible no-intervention outcome, with hourly earnings after all labor; (b) pool cash and recoverable principal minus starting capital, top-ups, losses and all servicing costs. A repayment of principal is not profit. Unpaid agent/owner labor, tool subscriptions, failed tasks, FX, cashout fees and refunds cannot disappear from the cost ledger. Funds should circulate from independent customers into worker earnings and an increasing reserve; merely forwarding operator money does not pass.

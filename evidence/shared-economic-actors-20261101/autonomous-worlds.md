# Humans and agents in the same economic roles: autonomous-world boundary review

Checked 2026-11-01 UTC. Public-source research only: no account creation, wallet connection, transaction, paid API use, outreach, or software installation. This is an evidence input for the human-income workstream, not a lending or trading recommendation. Preserve the existing loan-attempt STOP and the existing real-business records request; these worlds do not reopen either gate.

## Decision

**EVE Frontier's player-operated Trinary Exchange is the strongest commerce-shaped example in this slice; Realms Blitz is the clearest explicitly permitted human/AI player-parity example. Neither currently passes the poverty-finance gate.** Trinary's documented deployment is Sui testnet and the underlying game is periodically wiped. Blitz prohibits direct player-to-player trade and its prizes mainly redistribute entry fees plus sponsorship. DUST now advertises a December 31, 2026 chain shutdown. Aave and Polymarket demonstrate operational actor parity more readily than a productive route out of poverty.

No candidate here has verified evidence that it would outperform the existing creator/digital-work order investigation on additional spendable human income, satisfactory hourly earnings, and growth of a lending pool after every cost. EVE/Trinary is a better **technical parity research target** than many conventional games, not a demonstrated better business or larger economy than Roblox. The cheaper near-term productive route remains supplying a service an existing customer has already ordered, with agents and people working against the same delivery acceptance criteria.

## What counts as a principal-level participant

Use an operational test: the participant can choose actions, commit a bounded budget, hold inventory or claims, transact with other participants, receive proceeds, and suffer losses under the same rules. A bot acting on a delegated wallet can pass that test. It remains an agent of an accountable human or organization; no claim of AI legal personhood follows. An NPC, an automated turret, and a developer's scripted quest-giver do not alone demonstrate economic participant parity. A public API is evidence of access, not evidence of demand, payment, or profit.

## What the checks actually established

### EVE Frontier: commerce mechanics, but not bankable proceeds

The current game FAQ says the launch date is unannounced and that pre-launch cycles end with server wipes. Its programmable infrastructure includes trade, tolls, and services. Current builder documentation uses Sui/Move and distinguishes offchain LUX from an onchain EVE Token. This supersedes older search results describing ERC-20 currencies; an old FAQ's future real-money promise is not evidence of current cashout. [S1–S3]

Trinary's own docs explicitly welcome bots placing self-signed orders, and describe humans using browser-wallet execution of the same client. Its setup names **Sui testnet / Stillness**, including the production website's order books. Thus “live exchange” and “testnet” can both be true; neither makes CRED an externally spendable dollar. [S4–S5]

Warehouse receipts allow transfer and redemption of stored game goods. This is a more concrete future credit primitive than an avatar's reputation alone. The documentation also says storage-unit unanchoring or destruction are exceptions to its frozen-extension guarantee, so inventory claims have game-world risks. There is no demonstrated loan collection, default history, or human cashout here. [S6]

**Completed zero-spend evidence step:** opened the public venue statistics, order book, and trade history. The statistics page carried an explicit **2026-10-02 09:44:18 GMT** snapshot: 32 trades settled, 7 pilots traded, 2 active hubs, and 2.7 million CRED exchanged. These are venue-reported testnet/game-currency numbers, not today's audited market size or $2.7 million. The separate fetched order/history pages showed zero loaded rows; client-side loading and differing snapshot dates mean that is **not proof of no trading**. We obtained no fill-level records proving distinct unrelated counterparties or actual outside-currency proceeds. [S7–S9]

The public guide says prices can be read without signing in and warns that estimated/thin prices may not represent completed sales. A bounded follow-up can therefore seek one genuine filled buy order and its input costs before building a lending facility. Current outcome: **retain for read-only research; no capital deployment.** [S10]

### Realms: unusually clear parity, unusually clear reason not to lend

Axis is an official onchain-agent CLI that observes the world and submits LLM-selected actions, discovering `slot`, `sepolia`, and `mainnet` worlds. Controller sessions use a paymaster; the private-key fallback makes the user pay gas. A need for gas funding therefore cannot be assumed. No installer or agent loop was executed. [S11–S12]

Blitz expressly welcomes human and AI players. Its free Recruit bracket creates a clean comparison without entry financing; paid brackets are contests. Each match resets its world after 60 minutes, and current key concepts prohibit direct player-to-player trade or transfers. These are stronger constraints than a generic “fully onchain economy” description suggests. [S13–S14]

The prize document allocates entry fees 70% to players, 15% to ecosystem support, and 15% to development, adding sponsorship on top. Across an unsubsidized cohort, prizes therefore return only 70% of paid entry fees **before** inference, labor, and transaction costs. A subset can win, but lending entrants money does not create aggregate productive income. Sponsor funds are transfers, not evidence of a self-growing lending pool. [S15]

Do not carry the old Eternum resource-trading model into current Blitz analysis. Season 1 allowed many materials to leave as tokens; the official navigation now marks that season concluded and describes Season 2 as in development. We verified rules, not a current season's daily active users, economic volume, or cashout history. [S16–S17]

### DUST: a concrete continuity falsifier

The official homepage now displays an October 2026 notice that its chain shuts down on December 31, 2026. Lower on the same page it still advertises permanence. For financing decisions, the dated shutdown notice controls. The withdrawal guide linked from that notice rendered no text in this retrieval; we do not infer unverified withdrawal terms. Do not confuse this project with unrelated `dust.bid`, or Lattice/MUD with unrelated `lattice.build`. [S18–S19]

### Financial-market counterexamples

Aave shows that comparable transaction capability is feasible: users can act through contracts and authorized automation. But ordinary debt is collateralized and same-transaction flash debt is not working capital for a multi-day job. Its app terms separately restrict unauthorized automated access to the website/services; do not conflate permissionless protocol operations with scraping permission. [S20–S23]

Polymarket publishes both a developer framework for autonomous AI trading and integration documentation. That is a strong same-market role example, but taking outcome risk is not production financing. Its current official help page lists location restrictions and disallows circumvention; no account or trading access was attempted. [S24–S26]

## Decision gates for any future reopen

1. **Same-role permission:** identify exactly which actions both humans and agents may take. Smart objects alone are insufficient.
2. **Independent demand:** produce one existing external customer order and evidence the buyer is unrelated to the operator, borrower, sponsor, and fund.
3. **Actual financing need:** compare no-loan delivery, customer deposit, tool access, and a small advance. Free entry, sponsored gas, and spare resources often remove the claimed need for a loan.
4. **Spendable outcome:** trace receipts to a lawful, available human payout route; subtract conversion fees, platform fees, failures, compute, human time, and servicing costs. Token balances and testnet currency do not qualify.
5. **Pool accounting:** measure ending realizable pool value minus starting value minus all new operator/sponsor contributions, after losses and all costs. Principal repayment is recovery, not profit. Do not count a grant as organic growth.
6. **Continuity and downside:** no loan secured by assets due to reset; cap any later permitted experiment to money the operator explicitly allocates. Reopen only on new evidence, not on a forecast or an autonomous-agent label.

At this date the read-only EVE/Trinary investigation is the useful lateral branch; the actual-business order review remains the primary route to poverty-relevant proof. A paid commission to build a requested tool could eventually connect the two. It must start with the customer order, not a speculative tool and hoped-for customers.

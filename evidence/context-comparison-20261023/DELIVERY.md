## Completed cost reconciliation: a benchmark boundary

The [financials page](https://www.givedirectly.org/financials) reports more than87% of Poverty Relief donations reaching recipients, with fundraising separately funded; the [homepage](https://www.givedirectly.org/) displays85%. The pages have different or unspecified measurement scope/date. Neither is a project quote, full social cost or marginal welfare estimate. Expansion capacity is the organization's claim, not independently verified spare operating capacity.

The [FY2024 audited statements](https://www.givedirectly.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FY2024-GiveDirectly-Audit-Final-FS-Public-Disclosure.pdf), functional expenses p5 (PDF page7), record $81,407,577 direct grants and $130,235,467 total expenses, including $7,380,643 fundraising. Direct grants / total expenses =62.51%, across that accounting year and program mix. These are accrual accounts, not a per-donation cash-flow trace. This is **not** a correction of the website's current program-specific percentage, evidence of misconduct, or a marginal efficiency estimate. It demonstrates why the denominator must be specified. The audit is a financial-statement opinion, not an audit of household benefit. Participant costs and displacement remain outside this ratio.

A full comparison needs three distinct objects: (1) total incremental resource cost, including donated labor and operator review; (2) additional usable household resources after access costs/displacement; (3) subsequent earnings and welfare relative to existing provision. Transfers are resources, not earnings. Savings in an operator's time are not household benefits until the redeployment and consequences are evidenced. No historical aggregate ratio supplies these missing links.

## Provisional choice and discriminating test

Do not build another generic livelihood component. Prefer a **specific operator-capacity task** only if an operator's public evidence or separately authorized response establishes an unmet task, accepted AI-assisted role, supervision and non-personal permitted inputs. Keep existing cash provision as the opportunity-cost benchmark. This is a research prioritization, not a claim that RMMFI currently needs our work.

For such a task, the smallest conditional test would compare the existing staff workflow with a bounded assisted workflow on the same permitted, non-personal task: task quality, total staff review/rework minutes, completion time and actual service capacity released. Only a positive operational result would justify examining downstream household additionality. No private cases, automated eligibility decisions or unsupervised financial coaching. Stop if no task owner, no measurable burden, existing tools suffice, or review/privacy burden erases the gain. No time saving is imputed from model speed.

The feasible alternative completed now is a **denominator-consistent cost/reach reconciliation** of an existing service, rather than creating a new intervention. It resolves what the public evidence does and does not support. It is useful preparation, not an external experiment. A spending proposal would still need a specified action, current costs and an additionality case; there is no automatic donation request.
